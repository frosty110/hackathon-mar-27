# Persona 1: The AI Product Lead

## Profile

**Name archetype:** Carmen (based on Carmen Insignares Newell, who manually analyzed 125 AI startup pricing pages)

**Title:** Head of Product / VP Product / Co-founder (product-side)

**Company:** AI-native startup, Series A-B, 20-80 employees, $2M-$15M ARR

**Age:** 28-38

**Reports to:** CEO

**Team:** 3-8 product managers, sometimes a pricing analyst

## The Job They're Doing

Carmen owns pricing strategy. She decides the tier structure, the price points, the packaging, and the pricing model (per-seat vs per-token vs credits). She presents pricing recommendations to the CEO and board. She's measured on revenue growth, conversion rates, and gross margin.

Every pricing decision starts the same way: "What is everyone else charging?" She opens 15-20 competitor websites, screenshots the pricing pages, drops them into a spreadsheet, and tries to normalize wildly different models into something comparable. Per-seat vs per-token vs per-minute vs credits. This takes 1-2 full days.

By the time she presents the analysis, it's already stale. Two competitors changed pricing last week. She doesn't know because nobody's watching.

## Current Workflow (the painful one)

1. Open 15-20 competitor pricing pages in browser tabs
2. Screenshot each one
3. Manually extract: tiers, prices, features per tier, pricing model
4. Build a spreadsheet trying to normalize across models
5. Add a "notes" column for qualitative observations
6. Present to CEO/board
7. Revisit in 3-6 months (or when a sales rep says "Competitor X is cheaper now")

**Time spent:** 8-16 hours per cycle, 2-4 times per year
**Frustration level:** High. The work is tedious, the output is stale fast, and it's not her highest-value work.

## What She'd Pay For

- **Daily:** A dashboard showing all competitors' current pricing, normalized to a common comparison unit she defines (e.g., "50-person team, 1M tokens/month")
- **Immediately:** An alert when any competitor changes pricing, with analysis of what changed, the magnitude, and what it means for her positioning
- **Quarterly:** A trend report showing pricing movement across her competitive set (who's going upmarket, who's racing to the bottom, who added a new tier)

## Buying Signals (from the LinkedIn data)

- Carmen Insignares Newell (95): "We analyzed the pricing pages of 125 AI startups that raised capital after 2024." She literally did the manual version of Pricing Radar and got massive engagement. Her DMs were full of requests for the spreadsheet.
- Koshima Satija (92): Analyzed 200+ Voice AI companies. Found 12 distinct pricing models. Only 45% have public pricing. This is the normalization problem personified.
- Fynn Glover (95): "These are not annual pricing reviews. They are weekly operational decisions." The review cadence is accelerating beyond what manual work can support.

## Objections She'd Raise

1. "I only do this 2-4 times a year. Is continuous monitoring worth paying for?" Counter: You do it 2-4 times per year because it takes 2 days each time. If it took 0 days, you'd look at it weekly.
2. "55% of my competitors don't have public pricing." Counter: Knowing who's transparent and who's hiding is itself intelligence. And for the 45% who are public, you're currently not watching them at all between reviews.
3. "Can it handle my weird market where some charge per-seat and some per-token?" Counter: That's the entire point. The normalization is the product.

## Willingness to Pay

$200-$500/month. She has budget authority for tools under $500/month without CEO approval. Above that, she needs to make a business case. The value frame: "replaces 2 analyst days per quarter" = $3,200-$6,400/year in time savings, plus the strategic value of never being surprised.
