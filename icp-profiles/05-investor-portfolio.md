# Persona 5: The VC / PE Portfolio Advisor

## Profile

**Name archetype:** Mishaal (based on Mishaal Murawala, who analyzed the $285-300B SaaS repricing driven by AI pricing model reassessment)

**Title:** Operating Partner / Portfolio Advisor / Principal (Growth Equity or VC)

**Company:** VC fund or PE firm with 15-40 portfolio companies, many in SaaS/AI

**Age:** 33-48

**Reports to:** Managing Partners

**Team:** 3-8 portfolio operations team members supporting portfolio companies on GTM, pricing, and operations

## The Job They're Doing

Mishaal advises portfolio companies on pricing strategy. When a portfolio company is about to raise their next round, change their pricing, or enter a new market, Mishaal's team helps them understand the competitive landscape.

The portfolio-level challenge is unique: Mishaal doesn't track 15 competitors for one company. He tracks competitive landscapes for 15-40 companies, each with 8-15 competitors. That's 120-600 pricing pages to monitor across the portfolio. Nobody does this manually at portfolio scale. Instead, each portfolio company does its own competitive research (badly) and Mishaal sees the output once a quarter in a board deck.

His second job: pattern recognition across the portfolio. When the market shifts (seats to usage, flat to hybrid), Mishaal needs to spot it early and advise all affected portfolio companies. He's the one who should have told 5 portfolio companies "your market is moving to usage-based pricing" before they each discovered it independently in lost deals.

## Current Workflow (the painful one)

1. Portfolio company prepares quarterly board deck with competitive slide
2. Competitive data is usually 3-6 months stale and incomplete
3. Mishaal reviews, asks "is this current?", gets a shrug
4. If a major pricing shift happens (like the Figma AI credit rollout), Mishaal emails affected portfolio companies asking "does this affect you? what are you doing about it?"
5. Each company does its own research. No cross-pollination of pricing intelligence across the portfolio.

**Time spent:** Fragmented. 30-60 minutes per portfolio company per quarter reviewing pricing data that someone else collected (badly). No systematic monitoring.
**Frustration level:** Moderate but persistent. The real pain is reactive: he finds out about market shifts from LinkedIn posts, not from systematic intelligence.

## What He'd Pay For

- **Portfolio-wide:** A dashboard showing the competitive pricing landscape for every portfolio company, updated continuously. "Show me which portfolio companies are priced above market, at market, and below market."
- **Cross-portfolio pattern detection:** "Three of your portfolio companies' competitors added a usage-based tier this quarter. This is a market signal. Here are the three companies that should discuss pricing model changes."
- **Board-ready output:** A competitive pricing slide that's always current, that each portfolio company can pull into their board deck without doing manual research.
- **Early warning:** "Competitor X to Portfolio Company Y just dropped their price 30%. Portfolio Company Y may face pricing pressure next quarter."

## Objections He'd Raise

1. "Each portfolio company has different competitors. This is 40 different monitoring setups." Counter: Exactly. And right now, those 40 setups don't exist at all. Pricing Radar lets you create them in minutes, not days. The setup cost per portfolio company is 10 minutes (paste competitor URLs), not 2 days (manual research).
2. "I can't justify a tool subscription across the fund. Each portfolio company should buy their own." Counter: Two options. (a) Fund-level subscription that covers all portfolio companies as a portfolio operations benefit. (b) Recommend the tool to each portfolio company and they buy individually. Either way, cross-portfolio pattern detection only works if the fund has visibility across all of them.
3. "The data needs to be defensible in a board meeting." Counter: Every data point traces to a source URL with a timestamp. The normalization assumptions are explicit and adjustable. It's more defensible than the 6-month-old screenshots in the current board deck.

## Willingness to Pay

$2,000-$5,000/month at the fund level. Portfolio operations budgets at growth-stage funds run $500K-$2M/year. A tool that provides pricing intelligence across 20-40 portfolio companies at $3K/month ($36K/year) is a rounding error. The value frame: one portfolio company making a better pricing decision because of early competitive intelligence could be worth $500K-$5M in revenue impact. The tool pays for itself if it helps one company once per year.

Alternative model: per-portfolio-company pricing ($100-$200/company/month), billed to the fund.
