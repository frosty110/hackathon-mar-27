## Problem Statement

Every SaaS company does competitive pricing research manually. Product leads open 15-20 competitor pricing pages, screenshot them, build spreadsheets, and try to normalize wildly different models (per-seat vs per-token vs per-minute vs credits) into something comparable. This takes 1-2 full days. By the time the analysis is presented to the CEO, it's already stale. Two competitors changed pricing last week. Nobody noticed.

Carmen Insignares Newell manually analyzed 125 AI startup pricing pages and got massive engagement. Koshima Satija analyzed 200+ Voice AI companies over 3 months and found 12 distinct pricing models. Pricing consultants like Krzysztof Szyszkiewicz do this 15x/year for different clients, spending 2-3 days each time. Companies with 100%+ growth change pricing 3x more frequently (Wisam/Stripe), meaning the monitoring window is shrinking from quarterly to weekly.

The manual approach can't keep up.

## What Makes This Cool

An autonomous agent that does Carmen's 2-day spreadsheet job in 38 seconds. It watches your competitive set continuously, normalizes across pricing models, detects changes in real-time, and... here's the part nobody else does... acts as a **Pricing Architect** that reads your internal strategy docs and generates specific recommended responses grounded in YOUR positioning.

It doesn't just tell you "Competitor X raised prices." It tells you "Competitor X raised their Pro tier 60%. Based on your premium positioning strategy and Q2 growth targets, hold your price and emphasize the value gap in marketing. Here's the updated battle card."

The absence of data is also data. 55% of AI companies hide pricing behind "contact sales." The transparency map itself is competitive intelligence.

## Target Persona

**Carmen — Head of Product, AI-native startup (Series A-B, $2M-$15M ARR)**

She owns pricing strategy. She presents pricing recommendations to the CEO and board. Every pricing decision starts with "what is everyone else charging?" which she answers manually in spreadsheets.

What she'd pay for:
- A dashboard showing competitors normalized to a common unit she defines
- Alerts when competitors change pricing, with strategic analysis
- Board-ready output she can screenshot for her CEO deck

Secondary personas (same product, different use cases):
- **Krzysztof (Pricing Consultant):** Does this 15x/year. Saves 30-60 days/year.
- **Matt (CI/RevOps Lead):** Arms sales reps with up-to-date battle cards. His worst day: rep loses deal because battle card was stale.
- **Mishaal (VC Portfolio Advisor):** Monitors pricing across 20-40 portfolio companies.

## Premises

1. **Competitive pricing research is real, recurring pain.** Validated by LinkedIn data: at least 4 people in the dataset did this work manually and got significant engagement.
2. **LLM extraction can reliably parse pricing from raw HTML** for pre-selected pages using httpx. Not all pages, but enough for a compelling demo and MVP.
3. **Normalization across pricing models is possible** with explicit assumptions and a reference workload (e.g., "50-person team, 1M tokens/month").
4. **3 sponsor integrations + core logic + Streamlit dashboard is buildable in 8 hours** with the scoped-down approach (Ghost + Aerospike + TrueFoundry, no Overmind).
5. **Demo reliability is achievable** via pre-selected pages and a pre-staged price change for the detection moment.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Minimal Viable (Ghost + Aerospike + Overmind)
Single model for all tasks. Overmind wraps LLM calls for cost tracking. Simplest to build.
- Effort: ~6 hours core + 1 hour Overmind + 1 hour polish
- Risk: Overmind integration might look cosmetic to judges
- Completeness: 7/10

### Approach B: Ideal Architecture (Ghost + Aerospike + TrueFoundry) -- CHOSEN
TrueFoundry routes extraction (cheap model) vs strategic analysis (expensive model). Architecturally strongest sponsor story.
- Effort: ~6 hours core + 2 hours TrueFoundry
- Risk: TrueFoundry setup could eat time. Tight schedule.
- Completeness: 9/10

### Approach C: Belt and Suspenders (Ghost + Aerospike + TrueFoundry + Overmind)
All 4 sponsors. Strongest tool-use score. Zero margin for debugging.
- Effort: ~6 hours core + 2 hours sponsors. No margin.
- Risk: One flaky integration derails the whole build.
- Completeness: 10/10

**Decision:** Approach B. The "$0.04 scan cost" moment from Overmind is cute but low-value. What matters is the business value of the output and acting fast to respond to competitive changes. TrueFoundry model routing is architecturally meaningful. 3 sponsors meets the minimum.

## Recommended Approach

### Sponsor Stack

| Sponsor | Role | Why It's Load-Bearing |
|---------|------|----------------------|
| **Ghost** | Agent creates one Postgres DB at startup for the project. Each scan run inserts new rows. Ghost fork creates a snapshot before each scan for historical diff comparison. Fork latency must be tested in Hour 1 (if slow, fall back to simple row-based comparison with timestamps). | Remove Ghost and the agent has no persistent memory. The autonomous DB creation IS a proof point for agent autonomy. |
| **TrueFoundry** | Routes LLM tasks: cheap/fast model (Haiku/Flash) for HTML extraction, expensive model (Opus/GPT-4) for strategic analysis and Pricing Architect responses. | Remove TrueFoundry and you're using one model for everything. Cost goes up 10x on extraction. Architectural depth disappears. |
| **Aerospike** | Two separate sets in one namespace: (1) `pricing_profiles` set with vector embeddings of normalized pricing data for "who prices like us?" similarity search. (2) `strategy_docs` set storing internal positioning/strategy documents for the Pricing Architect to retrieve. Embedding model: text-embedding-3-small via TrueFoundry. | Remove Aerospike and you lose market positioning (the clustering visual) and strategy-grounded responses. The product becomes a table, not an advisor. |

**MVP scope:** 5-8 pre-selected pricing pages that work with httpx (no JS rendering). Scaling to arbitrary URLs requires headless browser rendering and is post-hackathon work.

### Core Features (priority order)

1. **Extraction Pipeline** — httpx fetches pricing pages in parallel (asyncio). LLM extracts structured data into a defined schema. 5-8 pre-selected AI company pages. Fallback: if httpx returns non-200 or empty body, serve cached HTML from local file and flag as "cached" in the dashboard.

2. **Normalization Engine** — Converts heterogeneous pricing models to a common comparison unit. Explicit assumptions displayed: "Normalized to: 50-person team, 1M tokens/month." When a pricing model cannot be meaningfully normalized (e.g., pure outcome-based pricing), display raw pricing with a flag: "Cannot normalize: outcome-based model."

   **Normalization schema (per competitor):**
   ```
   {
     "company": "Acme AI",
     "url": "https://acme.ai/pricing",
     "pricing_model": "per-seat",         // per-seat | per-token | per-minute | credits | hybrid | other
     "tiers": [
       {
         "name": "Pro",
         "base_price": 49.00,
         "unit": "seat/month",
         "features": ["feature1", "feature2"],
         "limits": {"tokens": "1M/month"}
       }
     ],
     "normalized_monthly_cost": 2450.00,   // for reference workload: 50 seats, 1M tokens
     "normalization_assumptions": "50 seats at $49/seat/month. Token usage within included limit.",
     "confidence": 0.85,
     "pricing_public": true,
     "last_scanned": "2026-03-27T12:00:00Z"
   }
   ```

   **Worked example:** Competitor A charges $49/seat/month. Competitor B charges $0.003/token. For a 50-person team using 1M tokens/month: A = $2,450/mo, B = $3,000/mo. Now they're comparable.

3. **Change Detection** — Compares current scan against previous scan stored in Ghost DB. Detects: price changes (magnitude + direction), new tiers added, tiers removed, model type changes (seat to usage). Change detection demo uses a locally-served mock page (Python http.server on localhost) that the team modifies live during the demo. This eliminates external dependency risk.

4. **Live Dashboard (Streamlit)** — Normalized comparison table. Transparency map ("7 public, 3 hidden"). Change alerts with magnitude. Market positioning cluster from Aerospike.

5. **Pricing Architect (module)** — Inputs: detected pricing change + internal strategy docs retrieved from Aerospike. Outputs: structured recommendation text with rationale, referencing specific strategy goals. Triggered when change detection fires. Uses TrueFoundry expensive model routing.

6. **"Who Prices Like Us?" Map** — Aerospike vector search clusters competitors by pricing similarity. Visual market positioning. When a competitor moves, the cluster shifts visually.

### 3-Minute Demo Script

**0:00-0:30 — Setup the pain.**
"Carmen is a Head of Product at an AI startup. Last month she spent 2 days in a spreadsheet analyzing 20 competitors' pricing pages. By the time she presented to her CEO, two competitors had already changed their pricing. She didn't know. This is Pricing Radar."

**0:30-1:15 — Live scan.**
Trigger the agent. Show it scanning 8 real AI company pricing pages. The Streamlit dashboard populates in real-time: normalized comparison table, transparency map ("6 public, 2 hidden behind contact sales"), market positioning cluster.

**1:15-1:45 — "Who prices like us?"**
Point at the Aerospike-powered cluster visualization. "You're in a pricing cluster with Competitor A and B. Competitor C is positioned 40% higher. Competitor D just moved into your cluster."

**1:45-2:30 — CHANGE DETECTION (the "whoa").**
A teammate modifies the localhost mock pricing page (changes Pro tier from $49 to $79). The agent detects the change within 30 seconds on its next poll. Dashboard flashes an alert with magnitude ("+61%"). The Pricing Architect kicks in, referencing internal strategy docs: "Competitor X raised their Pro tier from $49 to $79/seat. Based on your premium positioning strategy and Q2 growth targets: hold your price. You're now the value option in your cluster. Update marketing to emphasize the gap."

**2:30-3:00 — Land the impact.**
"Carmen's 2-day spreadsheet, done in 38 seconds. Continuously monitored. Strategy-aware. Board-ready. That's Pricing Radar."

## Open Questions

1. **Which 8 AI company pricing pages to pre-select?** Need pages that work with httpx (no heavy JS rendering), have diverse pricing models, and are recognizable to judges.
2. **What internal strategy docs to pre-seed for the Pricing Architect?** Need a realistic-looking positioning doc, growth targets, competitive strategy.
3. **TrueFoundry model routing setup time?** Unverified. Could take 30 min or 2 hours. Test in hour 1.
4. **Ghost MCP vs Ghost CLI?** MCP is cooler (agent manages DB via MCP) but CLI might be faster to integrate.

## Success Criteria

1. Agent scans 5+ pricing pages and extracts structured data without errors (demo path)
2. Normalized comparison table displays correctly with explicit assumptions
3. Change detection fires within 60 seconds of a pricing page change
4. Pricing Architect generates a strategic response referencing internal strategy docs
5. "Who prices like us?" clustering shows meaningful groupings
6. Full demo runs in under 3 minutes without breaking
7. 3 sponsor tools are visibly load-bearing in the demo
