# Persona 3: The Competitive Intelligence / Revenue Ops Lead

## Profile

**Name archetype:** Matt (based on Matt Green, whose exec revenue leader peer group debated consumption vs per-seat pricing, and Matt Price, who confidently funded a competitor trial)

**Title:** Head of Competitive Intelligence / Director of Revenue Operations / Sr. PMM (Competitive)

**Company:** Growth-stage SaaS or AI company, Series B-D, 100-500 employees, $10M-$80M ARR

**Age:** 30-42

**Reports to:** CRO or CMO

**Team:** 2-5 people doing competitive research, battle cards, and sales enablement

## The Job They're Doing

Matt's team arms the sales org. When a rep is in a deal against Competitor X, Matt's team provides the battle card: how X prices, where they're strong, where they're weak, and how to position against them. When pricing changes across the market, Matt briefs the revenue leadership.

His competitive intelligence program covers 15-30 competitors. He tracks pricing, features, positioning, funding, hiring, and customer reviews. Pricing is the hardest part because it changes without announcement, varies by pricing model, and requires normalization to be useful.

His worst day: a sales rep loses a deal and says "I didn't know Competitor X dropped their price last month." That means Matt's intel was stale. His credibility takes a hit.

## Current Workflow (the painful one)

1. Assign a team member to do a "competitive pricing sweep" monthly or quarterly
2. That person opens 20-30 pricing pages, screenshots them, updates a master spreadsheet
3. Matt reviews the spreadsheet, identifies changes, writes a summary
4. Summary goes to sales leadership and gets turned into updated battle cards
5. Battle cards distributed to reps via Confluence/Notion/Highspot
6. Repeat. Hope nothing changes between cycles.

**Time spent:** 3-5 person-days per cycle, monthly or quarterly
**Frustration level:** Very high. The work is repetitive, the data goes stale immediately, and when something slips through, his team takes the blame. He's spending analyst time on data collection instead of analysis.

## What He'd Pay For

- **Continuous:** A live feed of competitor pricing states, automatically extracted and normalized. No more manual sweeps.
- **Instantly:** Alerts within hours of a competitor pricing change, with a pre-drafted impact analysis he can forward to sales leadership.
- **Strategically:** Trend data showing pricing movement across the competitive set over quarters. "The market is moving from per-seat to usage-based. Here's the evidence across 20 competitors."
- **Operationally:** Auto-generated battle card updates when a competitor changes pricing. "Competitor X raised Pro tier 25%. Updated battle card attached."

## Objections He'd Raise

1. "We already use Klue/Crayon for competitive intelligence." Counter: Klue and Crayon track messaging, positioning, and news. They don't do structured pricing extraction and normalization. Ask them to normalize per-seat vs per-token vs credits into a comparison table. They can't. Pricing Radar is complementary, not competitive.
2. "How reliable is the extraction? If I brief sales leadership on bad data, I lose credibility." Counter: Every extraction includes a confidence score and the source HTML for verification. You trust but verify, same as any intelligence source. The difference is you see changes in hours instead of months.
3. "My competitors are mostly enterprise. They don't have public pricing." Counter: You're right that the deepest intel comes from deal-level data. But the public signal still matters. When Competitor X removes their pricing page entirely, that's a signal. When they add a new tier, that's a signal. And for the competitors who ARE public, you're currently blind between quarterly sweeps.
