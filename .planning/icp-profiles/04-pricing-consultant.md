# Persona 4: The Pricing Consultant

## Profile

**Name archetype:** Krzysztof (based on Krzysztof Szyszkiewicz, "The Pricing Guy," who talks to a dozen $5M-$50M ARR founders every week)

**Title:** Pricing Consultant / Monetization Advisor / Fractional VP of Pricing

**Company:** Independent consultancy or small advisory firm, 1-5 people. Serves 8-20 SaaS/AI clients simultaneously.

**Age:** 32-50

**Reports to:** Clients

**Team:** Solo or with 1-2 junior analysts

## The Job They're Doing

Krzysztof helps SaaS and AI companies set or change their pricing. A typical engagement: company is launching a new product tier, migrating from per-seat to usage-based, or just realized their margins are underwater. They hire Krzysztof for 4-8 weeks to analyze the market, benchmark competitors, build pricing models, and recommend a strategy.

The first week of every engagement is the same: competitive pricing research. He opens 15-30 competitor pricing pages, extracts the data, normalizes across models, and builds the comparison landscape. He does this for every client. The work is identical each time, just different competitors.

He's doing the Pricing Radar workflow manually, for money, 15-20 times per year.

## Current Workflow (the painful one)

1. Client names 8-12 direct competitors and 5-10 adjacent ones
2. Krzysztof spends 2-3 days scraping and normalizing pricing data
3. Builds a "competitive pricing landscape" deliverable (usually a spreadsheet + slide deck)
4. Uses this as the foundation for pricing recommendations
5. Engagement ends. The landscape analysis is frozen in time.
6. Next client, repeat from step 1 with different competitors.

**Time spent:** 2-3 days per client, 15-20 clients per year = 30-60 days/year on competitive pricing research alone
**Frustration level:** High, but he considers it part of the job. The real frustration is that clients call back 6 months later asking "is this still accurate?" and the answer is always "I'd need to redo the analysis."

## What He'd Pay For

- **Per engagement:** Instant competitive landscape for any set of 15-20 competitors, normalized and structured, in minutes instead of days.
- **Ongoing:** Persistent monitoring for active clients so he can provide "your competitive landscape changed" alerts as a value-add service between engagements.
- **Leverage:** The ability to serve more clients by eliminating the manual research bottleneck. If competitive research takes 0 days instead of 3, he can take on more engagements or spend more time on strategy (the high-value work).
- **Differentiation:** "I use an AI-powered pricing intelligence system" as a selling point for his consultancy. The tool makes him look more sophisticated.

## Objections He'd Raise

1. "The manual research IS my deliverable. If a tool does it, what am I selling?" Counter: You're selling the strategy, not the spreadsheet. The spreadsheet takes 3 days and the strategy takes 2 weeks. Cut the spreadsheet to 30 minutes and you have 3 more days for the high-value work. Or take on another client.
2. "I need the data to be perfect. If I present normalized pricing to a client and it's wrong, my reputation is on the line." Counter: Every extraction shows the source and a confidence score. You verify the numbers that matter (top 5 competitors), and the tool handles the long tail (competitors 6-20) where precision matters less than coverage.
3. "My clients' competitors are niche. Will it work for a vertical AI startup with 8 competitors nobody's heard of?" Counter: It works on any public pricing page. The normalization is the hard part, and it handles that regardless of how niche the market is.

## Willingness to Pay

$300-$800/month, or a per-engagement model ($200-$500 per competitive landscape generated). Krzysztof charges clients $15K-$40K per pricing engagement. The competitive research phase is 15-20% of the engagement value. If the tool saves 3 days per engagement and he does 15 engagements per year, that's 45 days saved. At his billing rate ($2K-$4K/day), the tool saves $90K-$180K/year in time that can be redirected to billable work. Even at $800/month ($9,600/year), the ROI is 10-20x.

He'd also want a white-label or "powered by" option so he can present the output to clients under his own brand.
