# Persona 2: The AI Founder-CEO

## Profile

**Name archetype:** Amelia (based on Amelia Wampler, who noted most AI founders do "napkin math" for pricing)

**Title:** Founder / CEO / Co-founder

**Company:** AI-native startup, pre-seed to Series A, 3-20 employees, pre-revenue to $3M ARR

**Age:** 26-40

**Reports to:** Board / investors

**Team:** She IS the pricing team. Maybe one co-founder who has opinions.

## The Job They're Doing

Amelia built the product. Now she needs to price it. She's not a pricing expert. She's an engineer or domain expert who suddenly has to make consequential pricing decisions with no data and no framework.

Her current approach: look at 5-6 competitors, pick a price that feels "in the ballpark," and hope for the best. She checks competitor pricing when she's about to launch, about to fundraise, or when a prospect says "you're more expensive than X."

She doesn't have a pricing analyst. She doesn't have time to build spreadsheets. She needs an answer in 30 minutes, not 2 days.

## Current Workflow (the painful one)

1. Google "[category] pricing" or "[competitor name] pricing"
2. Open 5-6 pricing pages in tabs
3. Glance at them, try to pattern-match
4. Pick a number that's "somewhere in the middle"
5. Ship it
6. Don't revisit until something forces her to (investor question, lost deal, margin problem)

**Time spent:** 2-4 hours, once or twice total
**Frustration level:** Medium. She knows she's guessing but doesn't know what else to do. The frustration spikes when a prospect says "Y is half your price" and she can't respond intelligently.

## What She'd Pay For

- **Right now:** "Show me where I sit in my market in 5 minutes." A normalized comparison of her pricing against 10 competitors, without her doing any work.
- **Ongoing:** "Tell me if anything changes." She doesn't want to think about competitor pricing until it matters. The agent thinks about it for her.
- **When it matters:** "A prospect just told me Competitor Z is cheaper. Is that true? For what use case?" An instant competitive response.

## Buying Signals (from the LinkedIn data)

- Amelia Wampler (96): "Most AI founders I talk to are doing napkin math to figure out their margins and how to set up pricing." She's describing herself and her peers. The napkin math is the status quo.
- Rishabh Goel (93): "If you get it right early, you compound. If you're still running flat subscriptions on variable-cost AI products, you're subsidizing your heaviest users." The cost of getting pricing wrong early is compounding quietly.
- Raj Khera (85): "Prospects are anchored to $20 consumer AI pricing when the real value is $3K+." Amelia encounters this in every sales call and doesn't have data to reframe the conversation.

## Objections She'd Raise

1. "I have 6 competitors, not 60. I can just check their pages myself." Counter: You can, and you do. Once. Then you don't check again for 6 months. Meanwhile two of them changed pricing and you found out from a prospect.
2. "I don't have budget for another tool." Counter: This is cheaper than losing a deal because you didn't know a competitor dropped their price. What's one deal worth?
3. "Pricing isn't my bottleneck. Product is." Counter: Fair. But when you do need to make a pricing decision, you make it in 30 minutes with no data. This gives you the data in 5 minutes.

## Willingness to Pay

$50-$150/month. She's bootstrapped or early-stage. Every dollar matters. But she buys tools impulsively when the value is immediately obvious (she pays for Notion, Linear, Vercel, Posthog). The product needs a free tier or a "first analysis free" hook. Conversion happens when the first alert fires and she realizes a competitor just changed pricing.
